Learn the Basics

Moneyline

The simplest bet: you pick which team wins. Nothing else matters.

Example: Hawks -150, Wolves +130. You bet on the Hawks. If they win by 1 point or 30, you win.
Reading American Odds (+ and −)

A minus sign marks the favorite: it's how much you bet to win $100. A plus sign marks the underdog: it's how much you win on a $100 bet.

Example: -150 means bet $150 to win $100. +130 means bet $100 to win $130.
Point Spread

The sportsbook gives one team a head start to make the game even. The favorite has to win by more than the spread; the underdog can lose by less than the spread and still "cover."

Example: Hawks -3.5. The Hawks must win by 4 or more. If you take Wolves +3.5, you win if the Wolves win or lose by 3 or fewer.
Over/Under (Totals)

You bet on the combined score of both teams, not who wins.

Example: Total set at 47.5. If the final score is 28–21 (49 points), the Over wins.
Parlays

You combine two or more bets into one. Every single pick has to win, or the whole parlay loses. The payout is bigger because it's much harder to hit.

Example: Hawks moneyline + Over 47.5. Both must win. If either loses, you lose the entire bet.
Props (Proposition Bets)

Bets on something specific that happens in the game, not the final result. Player props focus on one player's stats; game props cover events like the first team to score.

Example: Hawks QB over 250.5 passing yards. You win if he throws for 251 or more, no matter who wins the game.
Futures

Long-term bets on outcomes decided later in the season, like who wins the championship or an MVP award.

Example: Hawks to win the championship at +800. A $100 bet pays $800 profit if they win it all.
Live (In-Game) Betting

Bets placed while the game is happening. Odds change constantly based on the score and momentum.

Prediction Markets (Yes/No Prices)

On sites like Polymarket, you buy "Yes" or "No" shares. Each winning share pays $1, so the price works like a probability: 62¢ means the market sees about a 62% chance. You can also sell your shares before the game ends.

Example: You buy "Yes" at 62¢. If the Hawks win, you get $1 back, a 38¢ profit per share. If they lose, you lose your 62¢.
Implied Probability & "Edge"

Implied probability is the chance of winning that the odds or price suggest. If you believe the real chance is higher than what the market implies, you may have an "edge." This is what BetBrief's insights try to help with.

Example: Market price is 38¢ (38%), but stats suggest about 46%. That 8-point gap is the edge.
Vig (The "Juice")

The cut a sportsbook builds into the odds. It's why both sides of an even matchup are often priced at -110: you bet $110 to win $100, and the book keeps the difference over time.

Bankroll & Betting Responsibly

Only bet money you can afford to lose. Set a budget ahead of time, keep each bet to a small portion of it, and never chase losses. If betting stops being fun, call or text 1-800-GAMBLER for free, confidential help.
